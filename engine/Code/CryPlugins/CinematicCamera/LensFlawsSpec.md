What this addsThe field-dependent pupil made *out-of-focus* edges behave like a real lens. A cheap lens isalso bad where it is *in focus*: sharp in the centre, soft toward the edges, with colouredfringes on high-contrast edges. Three mechanisms, three parameters:| Mechanism | Parameter | What it does ||---|---|---|| **Field curvature** (Petzval) | `Field Curvature` (-1..1) | The focal surface is curved: when the centre is in focus the edges focus at a different distance. Positive = edges focus closer than the centre (the usual inward curvature), so a flat subject's edges fall *behind* the focal surface and go far-blurred; negative = the opposite. Independent of aperture as a focus error, but its visibility scales with the CoC like any focus error, so stopping down hides it ??exactly as on a real lens. || **Edge softness** (residual off-axis aberration blur) | `Edge Softness` (0..1) | Even the "in focus" edge is blurred wide open (spherical/oblique aberrations); vanishes stopped down. Implemented as a far-layer CoC floor `??p簡 |f|簡`. || **Lateral chromatic aberration** | `Chromatic Aberration` (0..1) | Magnification depends on wavelength: red and blue are imaged at slightly different radial scales than green. Zero at the centre, growing with field height; red/cyan fringes on contrast edges. Implemented as wavelength-dependent radial remap in the PostAA composite ??it IS distortion, per channel, so it lives in the same coordinate chain as `r_LensDistortion` and composes with squeeze and distortion for free. |The stock `r_ChromaticAberration` (a separate sharpening/CA full-screen pass in`GraphicsPipeline/PostEffects.cpp`) is left untouched and unused: it would cost a pass, and itwould not follow the `tcFinal` remap.Bold: at 1, wide open, the corners of a 1080p frame are visibly soft (edge softness) and fringedby ~4?? px (CA).
