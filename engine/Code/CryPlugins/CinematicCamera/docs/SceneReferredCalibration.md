# Scene-Referred Calibration ProceduresMeasurements that cannot be derived from the engine's source and have to be made once, in arunning engine, and recorded. Everything here belongs to Stage S2 of `SceneReferredSpec.md`.Nothing in this document is a code change. Each procedure ends in a number you write into a cvaror into a `.env` preset.---## 0. The instrument`r_HDRDebug 1` draws a stack of lines in the top-left corner:```Average Luminance (cd/m2): ...Estimated Illuminance (lux): ...Scene-referred EV: ...  (metered EV: ...)  pre-exposure x...Skybox: authored N lux -> renderer N cd/m2 (implies N lux, ratio N.NNx)  meter histogram [ ..:-=+*#%@#*+=-:.. ] CentreWeighted   -14..+18 stops over grey, 0.50/bin  band L..H (N..N cd/m2) at exposure X -> target EV T, applied EV E  pre-exposure reaches: sun+lights YES  probes YES  sky YES  forward/emissive YES  NOT reached (S8): SVOGI ...  global fog ...  volumetric fog sun/height ...```* **Estimated Illuminance** is a whole-frame average read back from the engine's luminance-measure  pass. It is a frame average, not a per-direction measurement, so a test scene must fill the  frame with the thing being measured.* **Skybox** is the S2 unit readout. `authored` is the "Skybox / Intensity (lux)" you typed in the  time-of-day panel, recovered exactly. `implies` is the illuminance a uniform sky of the  luminance the renderer is actually using would deliver on a horizontal surface, `E = pi * L`.  With correct units the two are equal and `ratio` reads `1.00x`.* **meter histogram** is what the camera is actually metering (S8,  `scene-notes/decisions/s8-metering.md`). One character per bin, 64 bins of half a stop each from  14 stops below mid grey to 18 above, log-scaled against the fullest bin so the tails still show.  Reading it:  * a broad hump straddling the middle is a scene the meter can read;  * a spike hard against the **right** edge is the sun (or an overflowed texel) in the saturating    top bin - the band discards it, and panning past the sun should therefore be a non-event;  * a spike hard **left** is a black frame or a lost loop;  * two separated humps is an indoor/outdoor frame, and is precisely the case where **Metering    Band Low / High** decide which of the two the camera exposes for.* **band L..H at exposure X** is what the camera made of those bins: the absolute luminance at each  edge of the percentile band, the pre-exposure the sample was measured at, the EV the band asks  for and the EV the camera settled on. Reading it:  * `target EV` far from `applied EV` and not closing means the camera is pinned at Auto EV    Min/Max, or Auto Exposure Lock is on;  * the band moving with the aperture instead of staying put means the pre-exposure is not being    divided out and the loop is a feedback loop;  * `[BAND REFUSED - holding]` means the band resolved entirely inside a saturating bin, or left    the plausibility window, and AUTO is holding its last EV rather than acting on it.* On `r_SceneReferredMeterHistogram 0` those two lines are replaced by the old log-mean pair,  `meter raw R / exposure X = A (N lux)` and the meter ceiling line, which are the AUTO loop laid  out end to end for the fallback meter: `raw` is the meter texture as it came back, in  PRE-EXPOSED units; `exposure` is the pre-exposure that was in force for the frame that sample  was measured on, recorded when the download was issued; `absolute` is the quotient, and it is  the only number the camera ever sees. Reading it:  * `absolute` should barely move when you change the aperture. If it moves WITH the exposure, the    division is not working and the loop is a feedback loop.  * `raw` collapsing towards zero while the picture is bright means taps are being read as black.  * `[SAMPLE REFUSED - holding]` means the measurement fell outside the plausibility window and    AUTO is holding its last EV rather than acting on it.* The two **coverage** lines say which contributors the pre-exposure actually reaches, computed  from the live switch and cvar state. A cross does not mean broken - it means "still on the S8  list". They exist for one symptom: *the aperture moves the sunlit surfaces and barely moves the  shadows*. That happens when the thing filling the shadows is the one thing not following the  exposure, and nothing in the picture explains it. SVOGI is the usual culprit (in integration  modes 1 and 2 it REPLACES the pre-exposed probe diffuse rather than adding to it), global fog is  the next. The A/B is two lines: `e_svoTI_Apply 0` and `e_Fog 0`; if the shadows start following  the aperture after either, that one is the filler. `e_VolumetricFog` requires a restart and  cannot be tested live. Ambient light ENTITIES are marked n/a on purpose: their colour modulates  an already-exposed accumulator, so exposing it would apply the exposure twice.* **meter ceiling** belongs to the fallback meter only and is only printed there. It is where the  log-mean meter saturates, in pre-exposed units and in stops above mid grey; a texel brighter  than this reads as exactly this, the way an incident meter's dome saturates, which is what stops  a sun disc past fp16's range from being read as black  (`scene-notes/decisions/s8-meter-saturation.md`). The histogram meter needs no such ceiling: an  overflowed texel lands in the top bin, where it has a position and no magnitude, and the  percentile band discards it.* The Skybox line appends `[no sky texture: term unused]` when the sky material has no diffuse  texture. In that state the skybox path is not drawn at all and the numbers are informational  only ??this is exactly why the times-ten went unnoticed in stock CRYENGINE.---## 1. That was wrong, and thecorrection is the point of the rewrite.** The Nishita model is physically formed: its phasefunctions are normalised to mean 1 over the sphere and pair exactly with a `4*PI*K` extinction, so`K` is a scattering coefficient divided by `4*PI` and the integral the engine evaluates is thesingle-scattering integral with no missing normalisation. Its output therefore carries whateverunit `m_sunIntensity` carries, and the renderer reads that output as an engine radiance:> **1.0 of the Nishita output = 10 000 cd/m2, and the `Sky Light / Sun intensity multiplier` is> implicitly in units of 10 000 lux of top-of-atmosphere normal illuminance.****The shipped 50 therefore asserts E0 = 500 000 lux against a physical 133 000 - it is 1.91 stopshot**, and that single number is the whole of the discrepancy. The scattering coefficients are aseparate matter: `beta_R` is 2.37x too large in all three channels at once, so the dome's HUE isright and only its level is wrong.**What the derivation predicts, sun at 45 degrees, shipped defaults.**| quantity | predicted | anchor | residual ||---|---|---|---|| zenith luminance | 5 700 cd/m2 | 8 000 (bright hazy) | -0.49 stops || sky illuminance, horizontal | ~32 000 lux | 12 000 (clear sky) | +1.4 stops |The two anchors are 1.9 stops apart and one scalar cannot satisfy both. That gap is the model'sown honest error, not an arithmetic mistake: single scattering underfills the zenith while the`1/cos` horizon brightening overfills the horizon, and a real sky saturates near the horizonbecause of multiple scattering, aerosol extinction and ground reflection. It belongs in thisdocument, not in the constant.**The anchor is ILLUMINANCE, and the shipped default is `r_SceneReferredNishitaScale = 0.37`**= `12 000 / 32 000`. Three reasons: `r_HDRDebug 1` measures illuminance and only illuminance;illuminance is what lights the scene and what a probe bake integrates; and an over-bright skyilluminance is exactly what flattens the sun/shade ratio the S2 acceptance test measures. Theresulting dome has a zenith of about 2 100 cd/m2 and a sky illuminance of 12 000 lux, with animplied E0 of 185 000 lux - still 0.48 stops hot, which is the single-scattering deficit beingpaid for openly.For the record, the alternatives: anchoring on 15 000 lux gives 0.47; anchoring on a zenith of8 000 cd/m2 gives 1.40 and puts the illuminance 1.9 stops over; leaving it at 1.0 is 0.5 stopsdark at the zenith and 1.4 stops hot in illuminance - defensible, and strictly better than aguessed constant, if the measurement below cannot be run.**The prediction is not a substitute for the measurement.** The steps below carry perhaps0.3 stops of uncertainty either way. Sun shaft strength (`Sun Shaft Amount`)**Why it needs measuring.** On the stock path the shaft colour is normalised to unit length, soshaft brightness is `SunShafts_RaysAmount` and nothing else, and the working targets are UNORM sothe mask is a clipped silhouette. On the scene-referred path the colour is the sun's actualexposed radiance and the mask is a real luminance image, so the same 0.25 is several stopsbrighter and the parameter has become a scattering coefficient.**Physical bound.** A shaft is single-scattered sunlight through participating media. Its radianceis bounded by roughly `L_sun * sigma_s * pathlength`; for clear air that is a small fraction of thesky's radiance and far below the solar disc. A defensible ceiling: **shafts should never exceedthe sky's radiance in the same direction, and typically sit 2 to 4 stops below it.****Procedure.** In the section 2 test level with the sky at a known luminance, raise the camera'sown shaft amount until the shafts read at the sky's luminance, then back off 2 to 4 stops. Recordthe value. Expect the physical setting to be of order **0.01 to 0.05**, not 0.25.**Check under motion.** The composite now happens before the tone map, which puts an unclipped,high-contrast, camera-motion-dependent radial pattern in front of the temporal resolve. Pan thecamera across the sun and watch for highlight crawl before signing the value off.### 3.1 The atmosphere the shafts are conditioned onThe amount is only half of it. Recording resultsWrite measured values into:* `r_SceneReferredNishitaScale` ??into the project's `.cryproject` or the ReC preset's  documentation, not into code. **The correction is carried exactly once.** The equivalent  content-only route is to set the preset's `Sky Light / Sun intensity multiplier` to  `0.37 x 50 = 18.5` and leave the cvar at 1.0; doing BOTH is 1.4 stops dark. `ReC_Physical.env`  deliberately keeps the shipped 50 so that the cvar's default lands correctly on every preset,  ours included.* **Re-bake every probe after changing it.** The dome reaches lighting through exactly three  routes - its own pixels, the volumetric-cloud atmospheric tint, and probes baked while it is  visible - and a probe baked before the change is stale by exactly the factor. The `_rec`  filename stamp does **not** catch this: it encodes the PI / light-unit convention, not the sky  scale. There is no mechanism; it is a discipline.* **Never use a probe's `Diffuse multiplier` as a luminance calibration.** It scales the light  colour's ALPHA, and alpha becomes the probe's blend weight in the tiled shading attenuation -  not its brightness. A probe that needs a trim needs an rgb-only multiplier, which does not  exist yet.* `r_ShaftAtmosphereRefLength` ??into the project's `.cryproject`, and the number and the frames  it was fitted against into `scene-notes/research/s8-sun-shafts-condition.md`. Until that  happens the shipped 500 m stays labelled as an assumption in the cvar help, here, and in the  source. Do **not** quietly promote it to a fact by deleting the label.* Sky and sun lux ??into the `.env` preset (`ReC_Physical.env`), never into `default.env` and  never into `EnvironmentPreset.cpp`, whose defaults and ranges are shared with the stock path.* The residuals and the date ??into `scene-notes/research/s2-*.md`, so the next session does not  re-derive them.
