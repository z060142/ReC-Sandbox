# Scene-Referred ContentWhat changes for the person building a level once the Cinematic Camera's **Scene Referred**property is on, what ships with the plugin to make that easier, and the two procedures ??thephysical time-of-day preset and the probe bake ??that a ReC scene needs.The engine's own path is untouched. With **Scene Referred** off the renderer is byte for byte thestock engine. Everything below is about the other path.---## 1. The one thing to know first**A level looks different with the camera on, and that is by design.** Not a bug, not aregression, and not something a setting will make go away.The reason is units. CRYENGINE's sun is divided by pi on the light side to make up for a `1/pi`that its diffuse and specular lobes both omit, while local lights are left alone. The result isthat a point light is 1.65 stops brighter, relative to the sun, than physics says it should be ??which is why the shipped defaults put the sun only 1.9 stops above a default point light when thehonest ratio is 3.6. The scene-referred path fixes it at the source: every light is radiometricand the `1/pi` goes back into the BRDF where it belongs.What that means for content already in your level:| Source | On the scene-referred path ||---|---|| Sun | unchanged || Point, projector, area light | **1.65 stops darker** || Ambient light (`DLF_AMBIENT`) | unchanged (it is a tint, not a radiance) || Environment probe | unchanged by the convention, but its *contents* are now stale ??see section 4 || Emissive material | unchanged || Skybox with an HDR texture | **3.32 stops darker**, and now delivers exactly its authored lux || Sun shafts | now follow the sun, the aperture and ND instead of sitting at a fixed display fraction |So a sun-lit exterior gets more contrast between sun and shade, and a point-lit interior drops1.65 stops against its sky-derived ambient. **ReC scenes are lit for the ReC path.** Re-lightingan existing level means raising local lights by about 1.65 stops (a factor of pi) if you want theold balance back ??but the point of the exercise is that you probably do not, because the oldbalance was not a real one.Two more, less obvious:* **Area lights are not corrected yet.** They go through an LTC integrator that never calls the  shared specular BRDF, so they do not receive the `1/pi` and are now 1.65 stops hot relative to  point lights. Whether the LTC lookup already carries part of that factor has to be measured  before it is fixed; until then, treat area lights as needing a manual trim.* **Legacy `.tod` files are path-dependent.** A pre-3.8.2 time-of-day file is converted to lux at  load time, and that conversion has to know which convention it is converting *into*. Loading  happens before any camera has activated, so a legacy `.tod` opened in a normal session and then  viewed on the scene-referred path reads pi times (1.65 stops) too bright. The fix is to re-save  the preset with the camera active, or simply to author a `.env` ??the legacy format has been  dead since CE 3.8.2 and only `Editor/asset_thumbnail.tod` still uses it.---## 2. That is an *interior* exposure.Physical midday daylight is around **EV100 14.8**. The two are 6.2 stops apart, and no amount ofauthoring can close that gap without making the preset lie about its own units.The gap is closed the way it is closed on a real set: **set `ND Filter (stops)` to 6** on thecamera's Lens group. Then```EV100 = 8.615 + 6 = 14.615      L_sat = 1.2 * 2^14.615 = 30 100 cd/m^2E on a sunlit horizontal surface = 100 000 * cos(30 deg) + 20 000 = 106 600 luxL of an 18 % Lambertian surface  = 0.18 * 106 600 / pi = 6 108 cd/m^2exposed value                     = 6 108 / 30 100 = 0.203```which is within a quarter stop of the 0.18 the whole pipeline is anchored on. Trim with theaperture, the shutter or exposure compensation to taste; ND 6 is the coarse dial that puts you inthe right decade. Or switch the camera to **AUTO** and let it meter, which is what it is for.> **Documented deviation.** The S2 task asked for a preset that "opens looking right at the> camera's default Manual settings" *and* for a sun of ~100 000 lux. Those two are the 6.2 stops> above; they cannot both be true. The physical values were kept, because D1 makes real light> ratios the point of the exercise and the sun/shade acceptance test needs them, and the ND became> the single documented dial. If you would rather the preset open flat at the shipped defaults,> scale the sun and skybox curves down by 6 stops (a factor of 64) ??but then the preset is no> longer physical and the numbers in the panel stop meaning lux.### The sun/shade ratioThe S2 acceptance asks for at least 10 stops between sun and shade in a ReC scene. That ratio is aproperty of the **geometry**, not of the preset: an 18 % surface in open shade under the full skystill receives 20 000 lux, only 2.4 stops below the same surface in sun. Ten stops appears wherethe sky is occluded too ??inside a doorway, under a vehicle, in a room lit through one window.What the preset guarantees is that both ends of that range are real numbers; the level providesthe occlusion.---## 3. What the preset cannot fix on its own**The procedural (Nishita) sky is uncalibrated.** It is a third brightness system with nodocumented unit and no relationship to the photometric one that can be read out of the source. Ifyour level uses the procedural dome ??which it does whenever the sky material has no diffusetexture ??its absolute brightness is a guess. Measure it and set `r_SceneReferredNishitaScale`;the procedure is in `SceneReferredCalibration.md` section 2.**Sun shaft strength changed meaning.** On the scene-referred path the shafts carry the sun's realexposed radiance instead of a normalised hue, so the amount is now a scattering coefficient andits useful value is of order 0.01??.05 rather than 0.25. Calibration procedure in`SceneReferredCalibration.md` section 3.**Volumetric fog, volumetric clouds, SVOGI, snowfall and lit particles are now on scene units.**They were deliberately left running and visibly wrong rather than switched off, and S8 fixed themat the root. Three of those changes have consequences for content:* **SVOGI's voxel cache stays in unexposed engine units on purpose.** The exposure is applied  where the GI result meets shading. This means the `e_svoTI_*` amplifiers, the diffuse bias and  the propagation booster all keep the meaning they have on the stock path - do not re-tune them  for the aperture. `e_svoTI_SSDepthTrace` is held at 0 on this path.* **The sun SVOGI injects is divided back by PI** so that it lands inside the codec's 4.0 ceiling.  If bounce light hue-shifts as you walk from a bright exterior to a dark interior, that is the  ceiling being hit and it means something is injecting radiometric light again.* **Lit particles were 1.65 stops hot** on this path until S8 and are now correct. Smoke, dust and  fog cards authored against the old brightness will read darker; that is the fix, not a  regression.The stock LDR post stage remains deliberately inert (above).---## 4. Re-baking environment probes**Every probe in a ReC scene has to be re-baked.** A probe is a photograph of the scene's lighting,and the scene's lighting has moved: local lights are 1.65 stops down and a textured sky is 3.32stops down. An old probe is a picture of a level that no longer exists.### The convention stampA probe baked under the scene-referred convention is **byte-indistinguishable** from a stock one.The `.dds` has no version field, the resource compiler has nowhere to put one, and the two candiffer by up to 3.3 stops. So the stamp is the filename: ReC bakes are written as`<name>_rec_cm.tif` (and `_rec_cm.dds` after compilation), and the bake path adds the `_rec` foryou whenever the camera's Scene Referred property is on.At load, a probe whose name disagrees with the convention the frame is being lit under logs onewarning naming the probe. If you see```Environment probe 'Probe_Hall': cube map '...' was baked under the stock light-unit conventionbut the frame is lit under the scene-referred one - it is up to 3.3 stops wrong.```the probe needs re-baking, not tuning.### Procedure1. Open the level in Sandbox with the Cinematic Camera present and its **Scene Referred** property   **ON**. The bake inherits the 3D engine's convention from the live switch, so this is the step   that decides which convention the asset carries.2. Set the time of day you are baking for. Probes are baked from one moment.3. Select the probes and press **Generate** (or *Generate to default path*). Each writes a 16x   supersampled fp16 cube map, calls the resource compiler, and reloads the result.4. Check the filenames: they should now carry `_rec`. The probe entity is re-pointed at the file   that was actually written.5. Reload the level and confirm no convention warnings appear.Notes that matter:* **The bake is taken at unity exposure, automatically.** Cube-map generation forces the camera's  pre-exposure to 1.0, so the cube map stores unexposed radiance and the runtime tint multiply  exposes it exactly once. Without that the exposure would be squared, and BC6H is unsigned so  nothing could carry a compensation back in. Nothing to do; just do not be surprised that the  bake ignores your aperture.* **The bake is tone-map-free and post-free** ??that is stock behaviour and it is correct.* **Probes are pinned to 256x256 BC6UH.** The atlas is a texture array, so every slice must be the  same size. Raising the resolution is not a per-probe decision.* **`r_HideSunInCubemaps` only hides the *procedural* sun.** If your sky is an HDR texture with a  painted sun, that sun is in the probe, and it will show up as a bright locked highlight on every  reflective surface in the probe's volume. Arguably correct for a scene-referred pipeline; decide  deliberately rather than being surprised.* **The editor's "generate all cubemaps" script does not cover component probes.** Select them and  bake them; a re-bake that silently skips half the probes is worse than none.---## 5. Effect thresholds change unit (Stage S7, D11)Every highlight-extraction threshold the camera drives is compared against the HDR target, andafter the pre-exposure that target is *exposed*: a correctly exposed 18 % grey card sits at 0.18 byconstruction (D6). So an absolute threshold no longer describes a fixed brightness in the world -it describes a brightness **relative to the exposure**, and it changes meaning every time theaperture, the shutter, the ISO or the ND moves. That is the "pops in at f/6.4" class of bug.**With Scene Referred on, these parameters are read as STOPS ABOVE MID GREY.** Off the switch theyare absolute engine light values exactly as before, to the bit. One function does the conversion,at the six sites where a stored parameter becomes a shader constant:```threshold_linear = 0.18 * 2 ^ clamp(stored, -6, +16)```The stops are clamped, not the linear result, because two of these sliders (ghosts and the frontfilter) have a 1..64 travel chosen for absolute values, and 2^64 is not a number any frame contains.Nothing is serialised differently, no default moves and no CRC changes. What changes is what agiven number *means* while the switch is on.### 6.1 The parameters| Parameter | Bus name | Default | Off the switch | On the switch ||---|---|---|---|---|| Highlight Threshold (DOF sprite bokeh) | `Dof_User_HighlightThreshold` | 2.0 | 2.0 absolute | +2 stops ??**0.72** || Threshold (lens ghosts) | `Dof_User_GhostThreshold` | 8.0 | 8.0 absolute | +8 stops ??**46.1** || Halation Threshold | `HDR_Halation_Threshold` | 2.0 | 2.0 absolute | +2 stops ??**0.72** || Flare Threshold (anamorphic) | `HDR_Streaks_AnamorphicThreshold` | 3.0 | 3.0 absolute | +3 stops ??**1.44** || Star Threshold (front filter PSF) | `HDR_Filter_Threshold` | 6.0 | 6.0 absolute | +6 stops ??**11.5** || Streak / diffraction source threshold | `HDR_Streaks_Threshold` | 4.0 | 4.0 absolute | +4 stops ??**2.88** |The soft knee each site derives (a quarter or a half of the threshold) is taken from the *resolved*value, so it scales with it and the fade-in stays the same width in stops.### 6.2 Converting a setting you already hadIf a scene was tuned with an absolute threshold **T** and you want the same picture at the exposureit was tuned at, the number to type is```stops = log2(T / 0.18)```| Old absolute T | 0.18 | 0.5 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 16 | 32 | 64 ||---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|| **stops to type** | 0.0 | 1.5 | 2.5 | 3.5 | 4.1 | 4.5 | 5.1 | 5.5 | 6.5 | 7.5 | 8.5 |Read the other way, a stored default of 8 (ghosts) used to mean 8 absolute ??about 5.5 stops overgrey ??and now means 8 stops, about 46. The shippeddefault of **50** was a compensation for lights that arrived clamped and unexposed: at 1 the wavemodel deposits the pupil's *actual* diffracted energy ??about 7 % of the source into the wholepattern and half a percent into the rays.S7 divided the stored value by 50 on the grounds that "with the pre-exposure in place the sourcesare real again". Real, yes; **brighter, no.** At a daylight exposure the pre-exposure *divides* byabout 3.4. Invisible, and correct: nobody sees a sunstar off a desk lamp atnoon.
